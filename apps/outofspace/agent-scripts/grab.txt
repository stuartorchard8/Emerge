# C, and ESC. Copying something already in the world, and backing out of what you copied.
#
# The gesture the whole build editor now hangs off: point the inspector at a thing, press C, and you
# are holding that thing -- its kind, its metal, its settings and the way it faces. Put it down on
# bare deck and you have built another one exactly like it. Put it down on a machine of the same
# FAMILY and you have RE-TUNED that one instead: the settings hand over, the metal and the facing do
# not. See `paste.txt` for that half, which has three rules of its own.
#
# It replaced C-then-V on the hovered tile: two keys, an invisible holding pen, and a rule ("same
# kind of machine only") that could only be found by breaking it. C keeps its key and does the lot.
#
# ⭐ **C copies what the POINTER is over**, not what the panel is showing -- the two disagree the
# moment you look at one machine and walk to another. The inspector still decides WHICH LAYER of a
# tile is meant, but only on the tile it is pinned to; everywhere else C takes the topmost thing,
# which is what a first click of the inspector would have shown. And the panel carries a COPY button
# for the same act aimed at the tile it is describing.
#
# ⭐ **A copy that lands POINTS THE PANEL at what it took.** The moment you copy a machine is the
# moment you want its dials in front of you -- you are about to put another one down. A press that
# copies nothing leaves the panel alone.
#
# What to read in the shots:
#   `inspecting`   -- the DECK panel of a tuned furnace: the setpoint and dwell shown here are the
#                     ones that have to come out the other end. ⚠️ And the COPY button under the
#                     layer tabs, which is this key with a face on it.
#   `holding`      -- the build panel with FURNACE picked and, in green, "copied from a furnace".
#                     ⚠️ **And the inspector back on the tuned furnace**, which the line above put
#                     deliberately on the OTHER one: the panel followed the copy.
#                     ⛔ The green line is the whole of what tells a player they are holding a COPY
#                     rather than a plain brush -- a stamped brush and an ordinary one are the same
#                     entry in the palette and behave differently, so this line is not decoration.
#   `cursor-build` -- the copy over bare deck: CYAN, the ordinary "this fits" colour.
#   `cursor-retune`-- the same copy over the OTHER furnace: GREEN, and allowed. Neither cyan (it is
#                     not building anything) nor red (it is not refused). Three answers, three
#                     colours; this is the one that says "the tile being full is the reason it works".
#   `copy-built`   -- the second furnace on the deck, and its panel: the setpoint came with it.
#   `palette-open` -- ESC once. Still the build tool, nothing in hand, and the panel says so and
#                     names C. A click here READS a tile rather than building -- see the line under
#                     the palette.
#
# ⚠️ Every setting shown is read out of the machine's own DECK panel, never off the cursor: that the
# controller is holding a setpoint proves nothing, which was exactly the old clipboard's failure.

new
creative 1
camera centre 25 14 40

# ── Two furnaces, one tuned and one not ──────────────────────────────────────
material Firebrick
brush FURNACE Right
# ⚠️ Clear of the starter vessel's own machinery — `field build` is how those tiles were found.
place 22 13
place 30 13
run 20

# Turn the first one up. The dials are only reachable through the panel, which is the point of
# driving them here rather than constructing a tuned machine: this is the player's own path.
inspect 22 13 DECK
tune 22 13 temp 4
tune 22 13 dwell 3
run 10
shot inspecting

# ── C ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
# ⚠️ **Aimed, and the panel is deliberately pointed somewhere else first.** C reads the POINTER, so
# the untuned furnace in the panel is what a version reading the inspector would have copied -- and
# the shot below shows the panel back on the tuned one, because a copy that lands takes the panel
# with it.
inspect 30 13 DECK
hover 22 13
grab
shot holding

# The cursor, over nothing and then over the other furnace. Same brush, two different verbs.
hover 26 18
shot cursor-build
hover 30 13
shot cursor-retune

# ⛔ The same machine, aimed at its top-left CORNER rather than its anchor. The preview must not
# move: a hand-over is aimed at an object, so it snaps to the whole of the machine it would re-tune.
# Drawn off the pointer this was a second furnace hanging off the corner of the first.
hover 29 12
shot cursor-retune-corner

# ── Putting the copy down on bare deck ───────────────────────────────────────
place 26 18
run 40
inspect 26 18 DECK
shot copy-built

# ── ESC, one rung at a time ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
# Out of the brush, into the palette: still building, holding nothing, and the panel says what that
# state is for.
escape
hover off
shot palette-open

# Out of the palette, back to reading -- and the tile survives the trip.
escape
escape
