# A pump: gas moved UPHILL, out of a room and into a pipe.
#
# The difference from a valve is the whole point. A valve stops when the two sides agree; a pump keeps
# pushing past that until it stalls at Pump.STALL_RATIO times its intake pressure. Read against
# valve.txt: same plumbing, far more gas in it.
#
# Note where the gas goes and what nothing had to tell it. A pump delivers PRESSURE and no momentum
# at all — nothing says which way along the run to travel. The ordinary pressure force and projection
# work that out on the next pass, so the run fills from the pump outwards in both directions.
#
# What to read in the shot:
#   a copper pipe run with a pump set into it, its intake bar on the face it draws through;
#   the `pipe` field map should show gas heaviest at the pump and falling off along the run.
new

brush PIPE
drag extractor extractor+4 extractor+12 extractor+4

# Facing UP: a pump draws from the tile it faces, never from the one it stands on. Its own tile has
# no room air at all — placing any building displaces the air out of its footprint — so drawing from
# underneath itself would draw from a vacuum.
brush PUMP UP
place extractor extractor+4

camera centre 20 9
camera zoom 28
shot pump

echo --- early on a pump is SLOWER than a valve: it adds a fixed trickle, a hole equalises at once
run 300
expect pipeMass > 1000
expect pipeMass < 1700

echo --- but it does not stop where a valve does. This same run through a valve settles at ~3050g,
echo --- and a pump takes it far past that: the ceiling is the machine, not the pressure it meets.
run 1700
expect pipeMass > 5500
expect airVented = 0
expect airBalance = 0
expect airHeatBalance = 0

field pipe
shot pump-filled
