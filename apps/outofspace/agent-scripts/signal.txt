# The signal layer: a button, a wire, and a door that answers to it.
#
# This is the flight loop in miniature, and every step of it is visible on screen — which is the
# entire argument for the feature. The old wiring model would have produced the same behaviour with
# nothing to look at: a sensor on the bow silently driving a machine at the stern through a colour
# named nowhere in the world.
#
# What to read in the shots:
#   a thin green run threaded along row 12, DARK while nothing drives it and LIT along its whole
#   length once the key goes down — one value, everywhere on the circuit, in the tick it is set;
#   the button's face lighting with it;
#   the airlock's iris opening in the starboard wall, and the room emptying through it.
new
run 1

# The run, laid along row 12 — open deck clear of every footprint on the main line, which matters:
# `place` is a no-op on an occupied tile, so a button dropped inside the extractor's 5x5 is silently
# never built and everything downstream of it reads as a broken feature.
brush WIRE
# ...all the way to x=36, *under* the door. A machine reads the network on its own tile, so a run
# that stops one tile short reaches nothing — which looks exactly like a broken airlock.
drag 12 12 36 12

# The button at one end of it, bound to RIGHT.
brush BUTTON
place 12 12
bind 12 12 RIGHT

# And the door at the other end, in the starboard hull. The wall tile comes out first: `place` is a
# no-op on an occupied tile, so without this the airlock is never built and the shot is of a hull.
remove 36 12
brush AIRLOCK
place 36 12
wire 36 12 WIRE 1000

camera centre 24 12 26
run 2

echo --- nothing held: a dark wire, a shut door
probe 12 12
probe 36 12
expect airVented = 0
# (No assertion on impulse here: swapping the wall tile for a door lets one tick's puff out, so the
# idle ship carries a few units of drift. What matters is the sign and size once the key goes down.)
shot signal-idle

echo --- key down: the whole circuit lights and the iris opens
hold RIGHT
run 40
probe 12 12
probe 36 12
shot signal-held

echo --- and the ship is under way, because gas left through the starboard wall
# (`trend` is left out on purpose — it throws on a format specifier, on any world, unrelated to this.)

# What the shot shows, in numbers. The thresholds are loose on purpose — this is an illustration
# and the real assertions are relative ones in SignalInputTest.
#
# Two readings that look like misses and are not. `airVented` stays 0: it counts gas leaving the
# *grid*, and 40 ticks of plume is still crossing the void outside the hull — breach.txt learned the
# same thing. `airMass` barely moves for the same reason: the gas has left the room but not the
# world, and this counts both.
# `impulseX` is the reaction, so venting to starboard drives the ship to port and this goes negative.
# That single number is the flight loop: a finger on a key ends up as momentum in the hull.
expect impulseX < 0
expect airBalance = 0

echo --- key up: the door shuts in the tick the finger comes off
release
run 2
probe 36 12
shot signal-released
