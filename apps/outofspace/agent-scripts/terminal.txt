# The terminal: the machine that lets a player choose their conductor.
#
# `PLAN_power_network.md` increment 3c, decision 4. Charge crosses between layers ONLY where a
# terminal stands, which is the single place the electrical contact graph differs from the thermal
# one. Machines declare their own ends -- a panel's two, a cell's two arms -- and this is the other
# form: a standalone rod, bonding whatever stands on its tile because the player put it there.
#
# The scene is a panel wired to itself the long way round, with the BOTTOM LEG OF THE LOOP MADE OF
# RAIL instead of cable. Track is metal, so it conducts; it is a separate layer, so it is a separate
# circuit until somebody says otherwise.
#
# What to read in the shots:
#   `terminal-open`   -- three circuits in three tints: the cable off the panel's left terminal, the
#                        cable off its right, and the rail between them. No carriers anywhere,
#                        because the panel is open-circuit and drives nothing.
#   `terminal-closed` -- one tint over the whole ring. Two rods, and the loop is closed THROUGH THE
#                        TRACK.
#   `terminal-live`   -- carriers streaming round it, along the rail with the rest. ⚠️ They run
#                        against the conventional current, because carriers are ELECTRONS.
#
# ⚠️ The panel is FORSTERITE: a conductive casing is a parallel path between a machine's own two
# terminals, so a steel panel shorts itself and drives nothing -- §5.

new
creative 1

# A panel needs sky, and the inside of a pressurised hull has none. Open the roof above it.
brush HULL
remove 24 4
remove 25 4
remove 26 4

brush SOLAR
place 25 6

# Down from each terminal and along -- but the two ends stop short of each other.

drag 22 6 22 9
drag 28 9 28 6
drag 28 6 26 6

# What joins them is a belt -- the vessel's own, which runs along this row already and is IRON, and
# iron conducts. The drag only makes sure the stretch between the two corners is joined.
brush RAIL
drag 22 9 28 9

escape
escape
